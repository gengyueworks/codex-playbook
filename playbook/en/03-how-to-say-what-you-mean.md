# 03 How to Say What You Mean

Many first requests sound like this: “Write an application for me.”

Codex can write one, but it does not know what you are applying for, who will read it, which facts must remain, or how long it may be. The first draft is usually a starting point.

When I prepared a Codex for OSS application, I first inspected the form fields, asked Codex for a draft, asked it to expose the missing information, added my real story, and compressed each field to fewer than 500 characters.

## Ask for missing information first

```text
I am preparing an application for an open-source project.

Do not write the final answers yet. Based on the form requirements below, tell me which facts, numbers, and experiences each field needs. Separate “must confirm” from “can add later”.

Each text field must stay under 500 characters. Do not invent project data or include claims I cannot prove.
```

This exposes the parts you have not said clearly yet: how often the project updates, what evidence exists, what you personally did, and what a reader can actually see.

## Add your facts

Use a small fact sheet:

```text
Use only the facts below:
- Who I am:
- What the project does:
- Confirmed numbers and dates:
- Evidence of public or automated operation:
- What I personally did:
- What the credits would support:

First organize these facts and list anything that still needs evidence.
```

Keep your voice. A submission can become tighter without turning into generic marketing copy.

## Compress after the facts are stable

```text
Write one English answer for each of the three fields.

Requirements:
1. Keep every field under 500 characters.
2. Preserve my identity as a non-programmer writer and my actual use of Codex.
3. Use evidence instead of broad adjectives.
4. Do not add numbers, impact, or project results I did not provide.
5. Report the character count and list facts I must verify.
```

Then make small revisions: make it sound more like you, remove stock phrases, keep the image of a writer making public work in a coder world, and delete weak claims. One class of change per round makes the result easier to judge.

## Do the final fact check yourself

Before submitting, verify the numbers against project files or the live GitHub page. Check that “daily” is supported by run history, that “original” describes the actual work, and that the character count includes spaces and punctuation.

Codex can compress the writing. You remain responsible for the truth of a form, application, contract, or public statement.

## A five-line brief

```text
Who will receive this:
What I want them to do after reading it:
Facts that must remain:
Content that must not appear:
Hard limits: length, format, date, or link requirements.
```

Use this card for introductions, applications, emails, and project descriptions.

## Practice

Choose a short bio, email, application, or project description you wrote recently. Fill in the five-line brief, ask Codex to identify gaps, and keep the original, fact sheet, and final version together.
