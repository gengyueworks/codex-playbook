# 10 My Toolbox

The chapters give you a repeatable way to work: say the idea out loud, let Codex read and ask questions, make small changes, check the result, and save the method.

This chapter keeps only the pieces you are likely to use.

## A five-minute task card

```text
I need to complete:
Reader or user:
Material I already have:
Files or sections Codex may change:
Facts that must remain:
I will check the result by:
Do not do yet:
```

## Ask questions first

```text
I have an idea, but the information is incomplete.
Ask me the five most important questions, ordered by “the task cannot continue without this”.
Do not give the final plan or modify files yet.
```

## Ask for a plan first

```text
Read the relevant files and explain the steps you plan to take.
For each step, state what you will read, create, or modify, and where I should check it.
Give me the plan only and wait before executing.
```

## Ask for evidence at handoff

```text
When finished, report:
1. every file created or modified;
2. why each file changed;
3. checks run and their results;
4. facts or decisions still missing;
5. a failure list and next step if the task did not complete.
```

## Choose your route

New to Codex: do the exercise in Chapter 02, then read Chapter 03.

Already comfortable with assignments: read Chapters 04 and 05 and write your project rules.

Many recurring tasks: read Chapters 06 and 07. Run the process manually before creating a Skill or automation.

Frequent material work: read Chapters 08 and 09 and keep counts, sources, and failure lists for every batch.

## One final lesson

Codex becomes more useful as your material, rules, and experience grow. Each careful review adds a judgment you can reuse. Each documented repeated step saves you from starting with a blank page.

I think of myself as a bard in the coder world. Others ship features; I turn stories, knowledge, and ideas into public work. Codex gives those creations a path from the mind into real folders, repositories, and readers.

Start with an essay, a folder, or a meeting note. Let it make the first move, then finish the work together.
